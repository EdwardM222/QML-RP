\abstract
This dissertation investigates a fully quantum stacking architecture for ensemble classification on Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum (NISQ) hardware. Classical ensemble stacking requires measuring base learner outputs prior to their transmission to a meta-learner, a process that collapses quantum superposition and discards the amplitude and phase information encoded in the quantum state. A measurement-free alternative is proposed and empirically evaluated, in which multiple small Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) base learners execute in parallel on separate qubit registers of a single IBM quantum device, with their unmeasured output states routed directly into a quantum meta-learner circuit. Three experimental conditions are evaluated: a classical stacking baseline, a quantum meta-learner operating on re-encoded classical outputs, and the novel measurement-free quantum stacking architecture. All conditions are assessed across established benchmark classification datasets under noiseless simulation, noisy simulation, and real IBM quantum hardware, with Qiskit Runtime error mitigation applied throughout. The central empirical question is whether preserving quantum state information across the stacking boundary yields a measurable classification benefit, and whether that benefit is maintained under NISQ-era noise.

Quantum Machine Learning (QML) occupies an unsettled frontier in which the NISQ era has produced a family of hybrid quantum-classical algorithms, most notably Variational Quantum Circuits (VQCs), whose theoretical promise has yet to be confirmed in practice. A structural limitation shared by virtually all current QML pipelines is their universal application of a measurement step before any aggregation, combination, or further learning takes place. In classical machine learning, reading a neuron's activation leaves its value unchanged; in quantum mechanics, measurement is irreversible, collapsing the superposition state into a definite classical outcome and discarding all phase and amplitude information encoded in that state. This asymmetry between the two computational paradigms motivates the central inquiry of this dissertation: whether, in an ensemble of quantum classifiers, deferring measurement until after the meta-learner has acted on the full quantum state is both feasible and beneficial.

    \challenge{\item Individual quantum classifiers in the ICS study struggled to scale with larger sampled datasets: QSVM, VQC, and QNN frequently collapsed to zero F1 scores at 512 samples, while the stacked ensemble preserved non-zero minority-class performance~\cite{vasan_exploring_2025}.}
    \challenge{\item Variational quantum classifiers are described as sensitive to initial parameters and difficult to optimise as dataset size increases, reinforcing the need to control training complexity in VQC-based experiments~\cite{vasan_exploring_2025}.}

    \challenge{\item The VQC composition study treats ansatz choice as an empirical ``lottery'': architectures with similar depth and parameter counts can differ substantially, so evaluating only one template risks overfitting the dissertation's conclusions to an arbitrary circuit family~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
\end{itemize}



Ensemble learning is among the most reliably effective paradigms in classical machine learning, with stacking enabling a meta-learner to exploit the combined outputs of diverse base learners and frequently achieve higher accuracy than any constituent model alone. In quantum computing, ensemble methods have begun to receive attention, yet virtually all proposed architectures follow a measure-then-combine pipeline that is structurally identical to its classical counterpart: base classifiers produce classical probability vectors, which a classical or quantum meta-learner subsequently processes, discarding the quantum nature of the base classifiers at the point of measurement. This work proposes and evaluates a measurement-free stacking architecture, referred to throughout as Condition C, in which base VQC circuits execute in parallel on spatially separated qubit registers of a single IBM quantum processor, with their output states routed directly into a quantum meta-learner circuit without intermediate measurement. The complete classification pipeline, from input encoding through to final prediction, is executed as a single unitary transformation, with measurement occurring only at the terminal stage.

\section{Research Questions}

This dissertation is structured around three specific, falsifiable research questions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Does a measurement-free quantum meta-learner achieve higher classification accuracy than a classical stacking baseline under noiseless simulation?
    \item Does that advantage persist under realistic NISQ noise, as modelled by a noisy simulator and confirmed on IBM hardware?
    \item How does a quantum meta-learner operating on re-encoded classical outputs compare to the measurement-free architecture?
\end{enumerate}

    \result{\item Modified stacking improves over pure logistic regression across probe, DoS, U2R, R2L, and overall accuracy metrics, increasing overall accuracy from 81.53\% to a best reported 92.55\% on KDD'99 intrusion detection~\cite{demir_modified_2018}.}
    \comparison{\item Different combiner methods favour different class behaviours: logistic regression gives the best probe recall, decision trees give strong normal/DoS behaviour, and na\"ive Bayes gives the best U2R and R2L detection in this study~\cite{demir_modified_2018}.}
    \result{\item Boosting improves validation accuracy over a single QAUM classifier by 5.1, 3.1, and 2.0 percentage points on MNIST Digits, Wisconsin Breast Cancer, and HTRU 2 Pulsar respectively, while training in less than half the time~\cite{mcfarthing_classical_2024}.}
    \result{\item Bagging improves validation accuracy over a single QAUM classifier by 3.1, 0.6, and 1.6 percentage points on MNIST Digits, Wisconsin Breast Cancer, and HTRU 2 Pulsar respectively, with lower training time than the matched single classifier~\cite{mcfarthing_classical_2024}.}
    \comparison{\item Boosting is presented as preferable when training time is prioritised, whereas bagging is presented as preferable when higher final accuracy is the priority and more training is available~\cite{mcfarthing_classical_2024}.}

    \benchmark{\item The paper evaluates accuracy, F1-score, training time, diversity score, and robustness under 0--15\% feature noise, supporting a multi-metric evaluation beyond accuracy alone~\cite{liu_construction_2025}.}
    \result{\item On KDD Cup 99, the proposed QBoost variant reports 96.8\% accuracy, compared with 93.5\% for Gradient Boosting, 94.2\% for standard QBoost, and 95.7\% for LightGBM~\cite{liu_construction_2025}.}
    \result{\item Compared with standard QBoost, the proposed method reports a higher diversity score of 0.32 versus 0.25, a 37\% reduction in training time, and 89.7\% accuracy under 15\% feature noise~\cite{liu_construction_2025}.}
    \benchmark{\item SEQSVM is evaluated on two BBBP datasets with 1970 and 8153 samples, using accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, ROC/AUC, and confusion matrices rather than accuracy alone~\cite{akrom_bloodbrain_2025}.}
    \result{\item SEQSVM reports mean accuracies of $0.950\pm0.008$ on D1 and $0.920\pm0.011$ on D2, with sensitivity and specificity remaining similarly high after SMOTE--Tomek balancing~\cite{akrom_bloodbrain_2025}.}
    \result{\item The reported test-set AUC values are 0.95 for D1 and 0.92 for D2, suggesting good generalisation of the measured-output quantum meta-learner on the BBBP task~\cite{akrom_bloodbrain_2025}.}
    \comparison{\item The paper positions SEQSVM as outperforming its classical base ensemble models by approximately 3--6 percentage points in accuracy, sensitivity, and specificity, making it a useful benchmark for measured quantum meta-learning after classical stacking~\cite{akrom_bloodbrain_2025}.}
    \result{\item On the Adult income task, the random-forest baseline reaches about 84--86\% accuracy; the QSearch simulation works well when $p_{Good}\geq0.5$ or $p_{Good}\leq0.2$, while intermediate probabilities require further investigation~\cite{safina_quantum_2023}.}

    \result{\item The individual QNN reaches 0.63 accuracy and 0.72 AUC, while the final ML--DNN--QNN meta-model reaches 0.85 accuracy and 0.91 AUC, close to established IDR tools such as DISOPRED3 and SPOT-Disorder2~\cite{kang_amino_2025}.}
    \comparison{\item Permutation importance of the meta-model ranks DNN highest, then ML, then QNN, suggesting that the quantum component supplied complementary interaction information but was not the dominant predictor~\cite{kang_amino_2025}.}


    \benchmark{\item The paper evaluates the QEL classifier on binary subsets of Iris, MNIST, Wine, and Digits, reporting accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, AUC-ROC, and Brier score as predictive and calibration metrics~\cite{rathi_enhanced_2025}.}
    \result{\item The proposed QSVM--PauliFeatureMap ensemble reports high simulated performance, including average Iris accuracy of 97.50\%, MNIST accuracy of 96.75\%, Wine accuracy of 77\%, and Digits accuracy of 100\% under the reported ensemble settings~\cite{rathi_enhanced_2025}.}
    \comparison{\item Against Macaluso et al.'s cosine-based quantum ensemble at ensemble size $B=8$, the proposed method improves the shared benchmarks from 0.61 to 1.00 on Iris virginica versus versicolor and from 0.84 to 0.99 on MNIST 0 versus 9~\cite{rathi_enhanced_2025}.}

    \benchmark{\item The evaluation compares QBag against classical bagging with $k$-means and supervised decision-tree bagging on Breast Cancer, Iris, Wine, and Real Estate datasets, using accuracy for classification, MSE for regression, and standard deviation bands for stability~\cite{rathi_quantum_2025}.}


    \result{\item Increasing the number of internal classifiers improves accuracy and reduces variance for bootstrap and boosting, while increasing samples per classifier does not always help and can hurt binary tasks when the sampled training subset becomes class-imbalanced~\cite{tolotti_ensembles_2024}.}
    \result{\item Boosting with the quantum cosine classifier and standardised data achieves the best median accuracy across the tested methods, while stacking gives consistent performance across normalisation choices because each internal classifier uses its own normalisation~\cite{tolotti_ensembles_2024}.}
    \comparison{\item The ensembles mitigate both unsuitable data normalisation and repeated-measurement inaccuracies, providing evidence that measured quantum ensembles can improve robustness even without preserving intermediate quantum states~\cite{tolotti_ensembles_2024}.}


    \benchmark{\item The study evaluates SVM, ANN, QSVC, QNN, VQC, and Bagging--QSVC using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, confusion matrices, ROC/AUC, and SHAP-based feature importance~\cite{abdulsalam_explainable_2023}.}
    \result{\item QSVC outperforms the matched classical SVM on the Cleveland dataset, reporting 88.52\% accuracy compared with 85.24\%; Bagging--QSVC further improves performance to 90.16\% accuracy, 0.90 precision, 0.90 recall, and 0.90 F1-score~\cite{abdulsalam_explainable_2023}.}

    \motive{\item ECOC is motivated as a multiclass ensemble strategy that decomposes a $c$-class problem into several simpler learner problems, with class codewords separated by Hamming distance to support robust decoding~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \method{\item Each ECOC matrix column defines a partition of classes into superclasses and trains one base learner; final classification decodes the predicted codeword by nearest Hamming distance to the class codewords~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \theory{\item Desirable ECOC matrices maximise row distance between class codewords and column distance between learners, while avoiding redundant complement columns and constant columns; these criteria give a principled way to design decomposition matrices rather than choosing them arbitrarily~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \method{\item The paper generalises binary Hadamard-style ECOC construction to prime-base $N$-ary matrices, where entries use $\{0,\ldots,N-1\}$ and each column partitions classes into $N$ superclasses instead of two~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \result{\item For prime $N$, the constructed square matrices $M_k(N)$ of dimension $n=N^k$ have row and column Hamming distance $d_r(M_k)=d_c(M_k)=\frac{N-1}{N}n$, giving optimal total distance under the paper's definition~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \comparison{\item Deterministic $N$-ary ECOC matrices are most competitive with random matrices when the matrix dimension is close to the number of classes, because little truncation is needed and high Hamming distance is preserved~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \result{\item Across six multiclass datasets, ternary ECOC often outperforms binary ECOC, and classification accuracy generally increases as $N$ grows, although gains begin to taper after about $N>7$~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \benchmark{\item The experiments compare deterministic and best-of-1000 random $N$-ary ECOC matrices using decision trees and SVMs on Pendigits, USPS, Vowel, Letters, Auslan, and ALOI, with codeword lengths $0.5c$, $c$, and $2c$ where feasible~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \limitation{\item The method assumes $N$-ary base learners, so moving beyond binary ECOC can increase base-learner complexity; for the dissertation this supports keeping binary ECOC as the main setting and treating depth-3/grouped variants as optional extensions~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}
    \gap{\item The paper optimises ECOC coding structure for classical ensemble classification but does not study quantum base learners, VQC trainability, qubit budgets, or coherent stacking, leaving the quantum ECOC design question open~\cite{nguyen_optimal_2021}.}



    \benchmark{\item Radojicic evaluates RSM-VQC ensembles using F1 score and training time, with Friedman and Wilcoxon tests used to validate observed differences; this supports reporting both performance and cost rather than accuracy alone~\cite{radojicic_reducing_2026}.}
    \result{\item In the RSM-VQC FYP, the 25\% feature-subset setting is reported as the strongest overall configuration, statistically outperforming larger subsets, while maintaining or improving performance relative to a full-feature VQC~\cite{radojicic_reducing_2026}.}
    \result{\item The same study reports that RSM ensembling saves 54.8\% of training time on Drug Consumption and 90.2\% on Parkinsons Telemonitoring, while the low-dimensional Raisin dataset shows negative time saving, suggesting that feature subspacing becomes more useful as dimensionality grows~\cite{radojicic_reducing_2026}.}
    \result{\item Pinto reports that random member selection outperforms top-F1 and architecture-diverse selection by more than a factor of six in mean F1 gain, indicating that individual strength and architectural difference are weaker selection criteria than actual prediction disagreement~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
    \result{\item Pinto reports that stacking produces roughly three times the F1 gain of bagging, supporting the dissertation's choice to emphasise stacking over simple voting or averaging~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
    \result{\item Prediction diversity is found to be the strongest predictor of ensemble contribution, while ensembling reduces cross-family F1 variance by 68.3\%, giving direct evidence that aggregation can mitigate ansatz sensitivity~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
    \result{\item Shallow circuits in Pinto's study provide the best F1-per-second behaviour and tolerate simulated depolarising noise at $p=0.01$ without meaningful degradation, supporting shallow base learners as the default design choice~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Research notes.}
\begin{itemize}
    \limitation{\item Quantum RAM and persistent quantum memory are described as impractical for foreseeable devices, so near-term algorithms should not assume stored quantum datasets or repeated access to saved intermediate quantum states~\cite{resch_quantum_2019}.}
    \limitation{\item Quantum computing is presented as a specialised accelerator rather than a near-term replacement for classical computing, supporting cautious claims focused on controlled empirical comparisons rather than broad quantum advantage~\cite{resch_quantum_2019}.}
    \limitation{\item The proposed quantum ensemble of parameter superpositions relies on idealised state preparation, making it more theoretical than directly implementable as a near-term VQC ensemble architecture~\cite{schuld_quantum_2018}.}
    \limitation{\item Full quantum advantage for NISQ QML applications remains an ongoing challenge despite broad exploration across domains~\cite{du_quantum_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item The ICS stacking study reduces the original 129-feature problem to two PCA components and evaluates sampled subsets of 128--512 instances, so its reported gains should be treated as evidence for small-resource hybrid stacking rather than full-scale deployment~\cite{vasan_exploring_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The ICS quantum stacking framework uses measured posterior probabilities and a classical Random Forest meta-learner; it does not investigate measurement-free aggregation of unmeasured VQC states~\cite{vasan_exploring_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item Exponential speedups from algorithms such as HHL are limited by caveats including input-state preparation, full-output reconstruction, matrix conditioning, Hamiltonian simulation requirements, and prohibitive practical resource estimates~\cite{biamonte_quantum_2017}.}
    \gap{\item The review highlights that the true crossover point where QML becomes practically useful over classical methods remains unclear, making empirical benchmarking against strong classical and quantum baselines essential~\cite{biamonte_quantum_2017}.}
    \gap{\item Schuld and Petruccione do not study finite trained VQC ensembles, ECOC decomposition, classical stacking, quantum re-encoding, or coherent meta-circuit aggregation, leaving space for near-term empirical comparison of these ensemble designs~\cite{schuld_quantum_2018}.}

    \challenge{\item The survey identifies appropriate ensemble size, model complexity, and training speed as unresolved issues, which directly supports limiting the VQC learner-count search rather than treating more learners as automatically better~\cite{dong_survey_2020}.}
    \gap{\item The survey is entirely classical and does not address whether ensemble diversity, weighting, pruning, or stacking behave differently when base learners are quantum circuits whose intermediate states may be coherently preserved~\cite{dong_survey_2020}.}

    \limitation{\item Although AdaBoost.Q improves quantum classifier accuracy, the MNIST result remains modest relative to classical models, and the paper does not claim a definitive quantum advantage~\cite{song_quantum_2024}.}
    \gap{\item AdaBoost.Q improves measured quantum classifiers through probabilistic boosting, but does not investigate coherent measurement-free stacking where base learner states are preserved before meta-level aggregation~\cite{song_quantum_2024}.}
    
    \gap{\item Friedrich and Maziero mitigate trainability problems by summing measured expectation-value outputs from multiple shallow circuits, but do not study state-preserving coherent stacking across trained VQC base learners~\cite{friedrich_quantum_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item The method is evaluated in simulation and does not test whether the proposed ensemble-layer construction remains beneficial under realistic hardware noise or on larger multi-class benchmarks~\cite{friedrich_quantum_2025}.}
    \gap{\item Multi-chip ensemble VQCs avoid inter-chip quantum entanglement and aggregate measured outputs classically, so they do not address whether preserving base learner states through a coherent meta-circuit improves stacking performance~\cite{park_addressing_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item The authors identify identical subcircuits and non-optimised aggregation functions as limitations, suggesting future work on heterogeneous subcircuit designs and task-specific aggregation strategies~\cite{park_addressing_2025}.}

    \gap{\item The paper explicitly calls for future QML work to focus on whether the actual learning part of machine-learning methods, not only expensive subroutines, can be improved using quantum information processing~\cite{schuld_introduction_2015}.}
    \limitation{\item The experiments are classical simulations of probability amplitudes using MLP classifiers, not executions of a quantum circuit on real hardware~\cite{araujo_quantum_2020}.}
    \gap{\item The work supports training in quantum ensembles but does not study VQC base learners, ECOC decomposition, stacked meta-learners, or measurement-free coherent aggregation of trained base states~\cite{araujo_quantum_2020}.}
    \limitation{\item The modified stacking study fixes the number of selected models at ten and identifies statistical justification of model-count selection as future work, supporting a limited and justified search over ensemble size rather than arbitrary learner counts~\cite{demir_modified_2018}.}
    \limitation{\item The ensemble is validated only in noiseless simulation rather than on real quantum hardware, leaving hardware noise resilience and coherence-time benefits as future work~\cite{mcfarthing_classical_2024}.}
    \gap{\item The paper studies classical aggregation of measured single-qubit classifiers, but does not address whether unmeasured base-circuit states can be coherently aggregated by a quantum meta-circuit~\cite{mcfarthing_classical_2024}.}

    \limitation{\item The method is quantum-inspired rather than a hardware-executed quantum model, so its results are most useful for ensemble-design principles rather than direct VQC implementation evidence~\cite{liu_construction_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item The authors identify two practical limitations: random subspace selection can discard critical features in very high-dimensional tasks, and the diversity/regularisation hyperparameters $\beta$ and $\lambda$ still require manual tuning~\cite{liu_construction_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item SEQSVM is tested on a quantum simulator and uses a QSVM on measured classical base-learner predictions, so it does not test coherent aggregation of unmeasured quantum base states~\cite{akrom_bloodbrain_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The work provides a strong example of quantum meta-learning over stacked classical outputs, but leaves open whether a VQC ensemble can benefit from a measurement-free quantum meta-circuit that receives base learner states directly~\cite{akrom_bloodbrain_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item Safina's quantum simulation uses classically precomputed tree probabilities as amplitudes rather than running tree inference coherently, so it supports quantum probability-estimation concepts but not a full measurement-free trainable VQC stack~\cite{safina_quantum_2023}.}
    \gap{\item The paper proposes future use of Qiskit and amplitude estimation to obtain both the prediction and Class1 probability, but does not study stacked quantum meta-learning or training on unmeasured intermediate quantum states~\cite{safina_quantum_2023}.}

    \limitation{\item The model remains slightly below some IDR-specific state-of-the-art methods and does not explicitly include sequence context or environmental influences, limiting its real-world biological generality~\cite{kang_amino_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The authors identify the need for better QNN feature-importance methods, because classical SHAP values collapsed to zero for QNN features even when entanglement ablations revealed important interactions~\cite{kang_amino_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The work uses a QNN as one measured component inside a classical stacking ensemble, but does not study coherent measurement-free stacking between quantum base learners and a quantum meta-circuit~\cite{kang_amino_2025}.}


    \challenge{\item The authors state that practical deployment would require noise-aware transpilation, qubit-placement optimisation, and error-mitigation methods such as zero-noise extrapolation and readout-error calibration~\cite{rathi_enhanced_2025}.}
    \validation{\item Unlike many toy quantum-classification examples, the stacking ensemble is evaluated on a real-time imbalanced ICS power-system cybersecurity dataset with tens of thousands of original samples, making it useful as a domain-relevant validation point for measured quantum stacking~\cite{vasan_exploring_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The paper identifies future work on fully quantum ensemble architectures, hybrid quantum--classical stacking, and robust training on NISQ devices, which overlaps directly with the open design space around coherent quantum meta-learners~\cite{rathi_enhanced_2025}.}

    \limitation{\item The model is evaluated in simulation and depends on classical preprocessing, classical stacking, and measured quantum classifier outputs, so it does not validate coherent aggregation of unmeasured quantum states~\cite{xiong_hybrid_2024}.}
    \limitation{\item The authors identify incomplete optimisation as future work: Bayesian optimisation is not applied to QKNN or VQC, bagging is not applied to QKNN, and soft/probabilistic voting is left open because some quantum classifiers lack probabilistic output designs~\cite{xiong_hybrid_2024}.}
    \gap{\item The paper suggests designing measurement methods for probabilistic outputs from VQC-style classifiers, which is relevant to measured quantum stacking but still leaves measurement-free meta-learning unexplored~\cite{xiong_hybrid_2024}.}


    \limitation{\item Although the method is framed as quantum bagging, the reported implementation performs distance evaluation and cluster assignment classically because current qubit counts and noise make the full quantum clustering subroutines impractical~\cite{rathi_quantum_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item The framework assumes CQRAM/QRAM-style access after classical data loading, so any speed-up claim depends on a memory model that remains a significant practical assumption for near-term hardware~\cite{rathi_quantum_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The method improves robustness through unsupervised bagging and measured/classical aggregation, but does not address training a coherent meta-circuit on unmeasured trained VQC base states~\cite{rathi_quantum_2025}.}


    \limitation{\item The implementation supports only binary classification and uses small UCI-derived datasets requiring at most 15 simulated qubits, limiting conclusions about multi-class, high-dimensional, or hardware-scale behaviour~\cite{tolotti_ensembles_2024}.}
    \gap{\item The study is a measured hybrid pipeline: quantum classifiers are repeatedly measured and combined by classical ensemble logic, so it does not address coherent stacking of unmeasured trained VQC states through a quantum meta-circuit~\cite{tolotti_ensembles_2024}.}


    \limitation{\item The Bagging--QSVC experiment is simulator-based, uses a small tabular dataset, and aggregates measured QSVC predictions classically, so it supports measured quantum bagging but not hardware deployment or measurement-free coherent stacking~\cite{abdulsalam_explainable_2023}.}
    \gap{\item The paper adds explainability through SHAP after measured prediction, but does not investigate how to interpret or train a meta-learner acting on unmeasured quantum states~\cite{abdulsalam_explainable_2023}.}


    \method{\item The paper proposes a weighted homogeneous quantum ensemble for instance-based quantum classifiers with indexing registers: a control register indexes internal classifiers, data-selection circuitry creates different training-point and feature subsets in superposition, and learned weights are encoded into control-register amplitudes at test time~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \method{\item Ensemble diversity is introduced through controlled permutations over the training-point and feature registers followed by post-selection on an auxiliary qubit, giving each control state a different data composition while retaining quantum-parallel execution~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \method{\item The training mode measures the control register to estimate each internal classifier's output on validation data, learns aggregation weights classically with logistic regression, and then re-encodes the learned positive weights into the quantum circuit for test-time aggregation~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \result{\item Across 11 UCI-derived binary datasets, the weighted ensemble improves average accuracy over single quantum cosine, distance, and SWAP-test classifiers in statevector simulation, despite each internal classifier being weakened by data selection~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \result{\item Increasing the control-register size slightly improves mean accuracy on average, suggesting that additional coherent ensemble diversity can help, although the paper does not establish open-ended scaling behaviour~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \comparison{\item Cosine and distance classifiers perform best with standardisation, while the SWAP-test ensemble performs best without normalisation; this reinforces that preprocessing and feature scaling interact strongly with the chosen quantum similarity model~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \challenge{\item In shot-based local simulation, the weighted ensemble is more sensitive to shot noise than the single classifier because the weight-optimisation stage depends on accurate estimates of individual internal classifier outputs~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \limitation{\item The experiments are simulation-based, use small binary datasets, assume efficient indexed data access and state preparation, and rely on post-selection, so hardware practicality and scaling remain uncertain~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}
    \gap{\item The method is a coherent weighted quantum ensemble, but it is built around instance-based quantum classifiers rather than trained VQC base learners; it therefore does not resolve how to train a measurement-free stack formed by appending a meta-VQC to trained VQC output states~\cite{tolotti_weighted_2025}.}


    \motive{\item Ensemble QNNs are motivated as a NISQ-compatible alternative to one larger QNN: several smaller, trainable circuits may reduce qubit, gate, parameter, and training-sample demands while retaining or improving predictive performance~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \challenge{\item Increasing QNN depth improves expressibility but can worsen barren plateaus and hardware noise exposure, so layer count should be treated as a controlled experimental variable rather than increased by default~\cite{cerezo_challenges_2022,incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \method{\item The paper evaluates bagging and AdaBoost ensembles of QNN base predictors, varying feature ratio and sample ratio across seven ensemble configurations, with ten base predictors per ensemble and average or weighted-average output aggregation~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \method{\item The QNN uses an $R_y$ feature map, a layered $R_x$--CNOT--$R_z$--CNOT--$R_x$ ansatz with $3\ell n$ trainable parameters, a local $\sigma_z^{(0)}$ observable, Adam optimisation, and parameter-shift gradients~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \result{\item In the Wine classification experiment, ensemble models reached their accuracy plateau with about three layers, while the full baseline QNN required roughly seven or more layers to exceed 90\% accuracy; this supports testing shallow ensembles before deeper monolithic circuits~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \benchmark{\item The paper reports that random-subspace bagging can save roughly 20--80\% of the qubit budget, but this saving may reduce feature coverage and therefore depends on the data manifold and sampling strategy~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \validation{\item The work evaluates ensemble QNNs not only in noiseless simulation, but also with a noisy IBM Lima simulation and on the IBM Lagos superconducting QPU, making it useful hardware-oriented evidence for ensemble-based noise mitigation~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \comparison{\item Bagging is positioned as especially useful for high-variance QNN settings, while boosting is more suitable for high-bias weak learners; this supports comparing aggregation strategies rather than assuming one ensemble method is universally best~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \limitation{\item The proposed ensembles aggregate measured QNN outputs classically, so the paper supports resource-saving measured ensemble baselines but not coherent measurement-free stacking across trained VQC registers~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}
    \gap{\item The paper leaves open whether the resource-saving and noise-mitigation benefits of measured QNN ensembles persist when the base learners are kept coherent and connected to a trainable quantum meta-circuit before final measurement~\cite{incudini_resource_2023}.}



    \motive{\item Quantum kernel performance depends strongly on the feature map, and QEKs make this design problem more explicit by adding trainable parameterised layers optimised through kernel-target alignment~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \challenge{\item The paper identifies a ``gate erasure bug'': data-independent gates placed after the final feature-dependent layer cancel with their adjoints in kernel inner-product evaluation, making those parameters irrelevant while still adding circuit and optimisation cost~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \method{\item Three QEK layer-ordering strategies are compared: data-first $(F,P)$, data-last $(P,F)$, and data-weaved architectures where parameterised layers are placed between feature-encoding layers so that no redundant parameter layer remains at the circuit end~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \method{\item The experiments train QEKs with kernel-target alignment, use five qubits, batch size five, 5000 optimisation iterations, 25 trained models per setting, one to five parameter layers, and evaluate SVC accuracy on Hayes-Roth, Heart Disease, Seeds, and Wine~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \result{\item Accuracy generally improves with more parameter layers but tends to plateau around three layers on most datasets, suggesting that extra layers may add cost without meaningful performance gain~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \result{\item Data-weaved kernels achieve higher accuracies in most test cases with the same number of parameterised layers, and show stronger improvement in target alignment during training, indicating better trainability than the data-first or data-last alternatives~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \comparison{\item Data-first kernels with $p$ parameter layers are mathematically equivalent to data-weaved kernels with $p-1$ useful parameter layers because the final parameterised block is erased; data-last avoids erasure but does not consistently outperform data-first at larger layer counts~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \benchmark{\item For a ten-feature example using their ansatz, the authors estimate that data-weaved placement can reduce one-qubit gates by about 11\% and two-qubit gates by about 30\% compared with less efficient alternatives~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \limitation{\item The study concerns quantum embedding kernels for QSVMs rather than VQC classifiers or ensemble stacking, and is based on small simulated datasets rather than hardware execution~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}
    \gap{\item The paper shows that feature-embedding placement materially affects kernel circuits, but does not study whether analogous data-reuploading or parameter-placement choices change the performance of VQC base learners or coherent quantum meta-circuits~\cite{salmenpera_impact_2024}.}



    \limitation{\item The two supervisor-provided FYPs are internal student projects rather than public peer-reviewed papers, so their unique empirical findings should be treated as design guidance unless replaced by public citations or reproduced directly in this dissertation~\cite{radojicic_reducing_2026,pinto_composing_2026}.}
    \limitation{\item Radojicic's RSM-VQC experiments are limited to three moderate-dimensional datasets, three independent repeats, and a fixed 100-epoch training budget, so the reported subset-ratio preference should not be treated as universal~\cite{radojicic_reducing_2026}.}
    \limitation{\item Pinto's noise validation uses an analytic depolarising overlay at $p=0.01$ rather than real hardware execution, leaving open whether the same ensemble gains survive hardware-specific noise, finite queue constraints, and transpilation overhead~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
    \gap{\item Pinto identifies replacing the classical logistic-regression stacker with a VQC meta-learner as a natural future extension, but does not evaluate a coherent measurement-free stack in which unmeasured base states are passed into a meta-circuit~\cite{pinto_composing_2026}.}
\end{itemize}
